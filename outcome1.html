The concept of AI, or Artificial Intelligence, can be traced back thousands of years ago, long before the development of modern computers. In ancient mythology, people wrote about beings that were made out of clay, bronze, rocks, etc., rather than human flesh. For example, Talon, a giant made out of bronze, or Golem, a living being made out of clay. These beings, though different from modern Artificial Intelligence, shaped our interest and curiosity in artificial beings that can imitate and behave like human beings, making it possible for us to give birth to the modern AI that we know today.

Alan Turing, the father of Modern Computer Science, helped lay the foundation for the computer age with his paper, "On Computable Numbers, with an Application to the Entschidungsproblem," published in 1936. In it, he presented a theoretical machine that follows simple instructions to complete a task such as compute a math calculation or solve a puzzle. He described the instruction as "Turing Machine" and the machine performing the task as "Universal Machine." Although it was a theory, his paper sparked the evolution of computer science and AI. As of now, we know that the "Turing Machine" and "Universal Machine" are the code and computers.

Later in 1950, he published another paper called "Computing Machinery and Intelligence," with the theory that computers would be powerful enough in the future to think like a normal human being. However, the concept of thinking is difficult to define, so he then devised the theory he proposed with an experiment called the "Imitation Game," later known as the "Turing Test." This experiment involves a human judge communicating with two players, one human and one computer. The goal is for the judge to determine which participant is the computer. If the judge can't distinguish whether one participant is a robot or a human based on their answers, then the computer has succeeded. It was just another theoretical idea and hope of Turing, but the theory opened a door for human civilization, encouraging them and sparking their curiosity to advance computers to the point that they can "behave" like a human being. We can see that through today's AI like Siri or ChatGPT. They don't necessary think or understanding things the same way to a human did but the way they communicate and response is resemble human conversation.
