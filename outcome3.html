Search, as the name implies, is the process of AI exploring its search space—consisting of every possible path, solution, and state that it can evaluate—to reach its goal. Search often comes with five main components: initial state (your starting point), goal test (test if you are at your destination), action (describe the possible step you can take during the path), transition model (describe the result after you move), path cost (the total expense of actions you took to reach your goal). There are also some other important terms such as search problem, what you are trying to solve; search tree, every possible path AI explored; search strategy, the behavior of the algorithm; state space, all the possible states of the problem; and environment, how the path and state look. There are also three fundatemematal serach algorithm, which are Breadth-First Search (BFS), Depth-First Search (DFS), and A* Search.

For easier demonstration, let's have a example of a train station. We have eight section of this train station, A-H, and our search problem is try to get to section H. Our state space is how the layout of the train station looks like, which is the image below.

Breadth-First Search, or BFS, is a search that often used to find the shortest path to your goal. The search strategy or the way it work is that it search layer by layer and try to find which route would be the shortest. Our initial state will be section A and we will begin searching layer by layer, which mean searching the very first two section that connected to A, which is section B, E, and F. The legal action we can take here is to go to section B, E, or F. Using the idea of First In First Out, or basically first come first serve, and in this example, B will be explored first, then E, and then F. Our transition model will describe something like "We are currently in section B. For BFS, we will perform goal test after every action we took which in this case, none of the three station is section is H. So we continue explore and using that concept of FIFO, we will explore sections that connected to B first then E, and then F again.
